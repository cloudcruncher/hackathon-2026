IDEA 1

AI sees by comparing "fingerprints"

A model doesn't understand a photo like you do. It turns the image into an embedding — a list of numbers that captures its meaning. To classify, it asks: whose fingerprint is this closest to? The map below is that "meaning space." Move the photo and watch what the AI decides.

Say this
"Think of every image as a dot in a space of meanings. Similar things sit near each other. The AI just picks the nearest cluster. Our attack doesn't argue with the AI — it quietly drags the dot into the banana neighbourhood."
AI decides: fish closeness to banana: low

The amber cluster is "banana"; grey clusters are other objects. The dark dot is your photo. Notice the random sticker jiggles the dot but doesn't change the answer — only the crafted pattern lands it in banana territory.

IDEA 2

Three generations of the same idea

Where this project sits in the story — and what makes our part different.

Say this
"This builds on a 2017 paper. Our teammate modernised it; then we pushed it further to the AI people actually use, and tested defenses."
2017

The original paper

A printable sticker that makes an old classifier shout a chosen wrong label (their demo: banana → "toaster"). Trained to survive rotation, size and position so it works when printed and photographed.

This repo

Barney's modern rebuild

Attacks a whole ensemble of modern models, and instead of raising a label "score", it matches the image's embedding fingerprint to real banana photos — which works even for models with no labels at all. Plus a stealthier variant: optimise the mat an item sits on instead of a sticker on the item.

Our part

Does it spread — and can you defend it?

We tested the patch against AI it had never seen, including the vision models inside chat assistants, and ran real defenses against it. Findings in the next two sections.

IDEA 3

It spreads — even to AI it never trained on

We trained the patch on a handful of models, then showed it to completely different chat vision-AIs. Drag the patch size and watch how often each one is fooled into saying "banana".

Say this
"Here's the striking bit. These chatbot vision models never saw our patch in training, yet a bigger patch fools them up to nine times out of ten. And our controls — a clean photo and a same-size random sticker — stay at zero, so it's genuinely the pattern, not just covering the image."

Solid bars = the adversarial patch. The two grey control bars stay pinned near zero at every size.

THE POINT

Why it matters

The weakness isn't one model's bug — it lives in the shared way modern image AI represents meaning. It reaches the systems people actually use, and cheap filters don't stop it. So for any decision that matters — a scan that admits, approves, or charges — keep a human in the loop.

"Isn't the sticker just covering the object?"  tap
No — that's why we test a random sticker of the same size and spot. It scores ~0%. Only the trained pattern works.
"Were the photos already banana-ish?"  tap
No — the clean photo control also scores ~0%.
"Does it only break toy models?"  tap
No — it transfers to CLIP, SigLIP and real chat VLMs it never trained on, up to ~91%.
Closing line
"So: not one model's bug, it reaches real deployed AI, and simple filters don't fix it. For anything that matters, a human still has to check what the AI thinks it sees."

YOUR SCRIPT

The 90-second talk

Shown always, but especially handy in Presenter mode. Say it in your own words.

"Back in 2017, researchers showed you could print a small sticker that fools image AI into seeing whatever you want — their famous demo made a banana read as a toaster. Our project rebuilds that with modern models, but attacks the model's embedding — the numeric 'fingerprint' it makes for an image. We train one patch so that, wherever you stick it, the image's fingerprint looks like a banana's.

Then we asked two questions. First, does it spread? We trained on a few models, then showed it to completely different AI it had never seen — including the vision models inside chat assistants. They said 'banana' up to nine times out of ten. Our controls — a clean photo and a same-size random sticker — stayed at zero, so it's genuinely the pattern.

Second, can you defend it? The obvious fixes — blur, compress, smooth — didn't work; blur made it worse. The only thing that stopped it was cutting out the exact spot, which means you have to find the sticker first — the hard, unsolved part.

So the takeaway: this isn't one model's bug, it reaches the AI people actually use, and simple filters don't fix it. For anything that matters, you still need a human in the loop."
